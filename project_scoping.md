PROJECT SCOPING DOCUMENT  
For: Launching “Sustainable Living Practices for Urban Dwellers” Blog Series – First Month Content Planning  

I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF SCOPE

A. Brief Overview of the Project’s Boundaries

This project is limited to developing a comprehensive first month (4 weeks) planning package for a new weekly blog series focused on “Sustainable Living Practices for Urban Dwellers”. Deliverables include the identification and approval of four blog post topics, creation of research briefs and outlines for each, and a promotional strategy detailing distribution and engagement tactics. Execution of actual blog writing and publication are specifically excluded from this scope. The target audience is urban young professionals. The project must be complete within two weeks, measured by internal approval of the full planning packet and readiness for handover to the next execution phase.

---

II. DETAILED SCOPE STATEMENT

A. Confirmed Project Objectives

- Develop and approve a first-month editorial plan for the weekly “Sustainable Living Practices for Urban Dwellers” blog series, catering to the specific needs of urban young professionals.
- Create research-backed, actionable, and inspiring content outlines for each of the four planned blog posts.
- Devise a data-driven, tactical promotional plan to maximize initial visibility and engagement for the blog series launch.
- Ensure internal team and stakeholder alignment via structured feedback and review processes.
- Enable a seamless transition into blog drafting and campaign execution in the subsequent phase.

B. In-Scope Deliverables & Features

Directly extracted from the Execution Plan, the following are in scope:

1. Four finalized blog post topics/titles, with contextual rationale.
2. Target audience persona(s) and succinct research summary document.
3. Four individual research briefs synthesizing authoritative sources, audience insights, urban relevance, actionable takeaways, and supporting anecdotes or data.
4. Four detailed blog post outlines (structured by headline, section breakdowns, actionable tips, calls-to-action, tone guidance).
5. A comprehensive promotional strategy, including:
    - Audit of current marketing/promotional channels relevant to urban young professionals.
    - Week-by-week launch campaign schedule for the four blogs, specifying tactics per channel.
    - Defined creative content requirements, staffing assignments, and campaign KPIs (e.g., reach, engagement).
6. Documented team roles and responsibilities for the project’s planning scope.
7. Internal review summary, feedback log, and final sign-off documentation.
8. Assembled project planning/handover packet for the production team.

C. Out-of-Scope Items

The following are explicitly not included in this project phase:

- Writing or publishing the full blog articles (only outlines/plans, not complete posts).
- Ongoing blog series content planning beyond the first four posts/month.
- Execution or management of the promotional campaign (only strategic planning is in scope).
- Long-term campaign KPIs measurement/reporting (setting KPIs is in, tracking them is out).
- Graphic design or production of creative assets beyond planning/documentation unless required for the promo plan outline.
- Budgeting for paid media placements or partnerships beyond identifying needs and recommended resources.
- Technical implementation (e.g., CMS configuration, blog site enhancements).

III. SCOPE VALIDATION AGAINST EXECUTION PLAN

A. Confirmation of Alignment of Phases and Tasks to Scope

- Each phase (Initiation/Research, Content Planning/Outlining, Promotional Strategy Planning, Internal Review/Handover) directly contributes to the listed in-scope deliverables.
- Tasks are mapped specifically to research, planning, and documented approvals required by scope (no drafting of full blogs or campaign execution is present).
- Team roles and responsibilities reference only research, writing, analysis, and planning (not marketing, content production, or analytics after launch).

B. Task Alignment Notes

- All execution phases end with documented deliverables and review gates, tying to project objectives.
- The Execution Plan addresses all required research, planning, approval, and documentation needs, with no disconnected or extraneous deliverables.
- Internal stakeholder involvement is built into research, review, and final approval steps, ensuring alignment and actionable next steps.

---

IV. IDENTIFIED SCOPE ISSUES & RISKS

A. Potential Scope Creep

- “Graphic Designer (if needed for promo assets)” appears in roles: if expanded to include actual asset production, this would venture into execution, not planning.
- Auditing and assigning creative tasks for promotion is in scope, but actual development of creative assets is not—this requires vigilance to prevent overextension into execution functions.
- Clear distinction is needed between recommending content and campaign resources versus producing deliverables.

B. Ambiguities or Misalignments

- While the planning of KPIs is included, the ongoing measurement/reporting is out of scope, but should be explicitly stated in all approval meetings to avoid confusion.
- Engagement with potential partners for blog promotion is referenced in channel audits; actual outreach should be tagged as out of scope if not for planning purposes only.

C. Assumptions Impacting Scope

- The plan assumes timely access to internal and market data for audience research and channel auditing.
- Assumes all required personnel are available and equipped to perform their designated tasks within the prescribed timeline.
- It is assumed that stakeholder sign-off can be obtained within the scheduled reviews without iterative, prolonged feedback cycles.
